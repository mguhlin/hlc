Facilitator Guide

How to run The Cuban Missile Crisis, 1962 as Problem-Based Learning: your role, the pre-planning maps, pacing, role cards, sources, and debrief prompts. The golden rule — guide, don't tell. In PBL the students should feel they, not you, planned the investigation.

Your role by phase

PhaseWhat you doWhat you resist
① SurfaceTeach vocabulary and strategic facts efficiently; run the jigsaw; check acquisition.Rushing to the problem before facts are secure.
② DeepFacilitate the options–consequences map and source routine; model claim + evidence.Giving your own verdict on the decision or the sources.
③ TransferRead the problem well; hold the KWHL chart; answer questions with questions; point to sources.Answering the "Need to know" questions yourself.

Running the jigsaw (Phase 1)

The Surface phase uses a four-topic jigsaw (How did thirteen days bring the world to the brink?) — a high-leverage move (d ≈ 0.92) because every student must teach. The flow: expert groups each study one topic and take notes → students re-mix into home groups with one expert per topic → each expert teaches → an individual check for understanding holds everyone accountable.

Pre-planning · Map of Possibilities

Before teaching, brainstorm every direction the missile-crisis problem could branch — so you can steer discussion and decide, in advance, which threads are productive and which are too sensitive or off-topic for your class and community.

Center: The Cuban Missile Crisis, 1962. Branches: the Cold War & the arms race · the Bay of Pigs backdrop · the U-2 discovery · the ExComm options · the blockade / "quarantine" · the two Khrushchev letters · the secret Jupiter-missiles trade · Cuban sovereignty & who was at the table. Mark threads you will encourage and any you will redirect (keep it factual, professional, and age-appropriate — relevant, not inflammatory).

Suggested pacing (5–8 class periods)

  • Period 1: Surface — vocabulary sort + jigsaw.
  • Period 2: Surface map work → Deep options–consequences map.
  • Period 3: Deep — primary sources + warm-up argument.
  • Period 4: Transfer — Meet the Problem, roles, Hunches/Know/Need-to-Know.
  • Periods 5–6: Transfer — inquiry & investigation.
  • Periods 7–8: Transfer — build, present, and debrief the 8-part brief.

Facilitation prompts (keep these handy)

  • "What in the record makes you say that?"
  • "Where could we find that out? Which source?"
  • "How do you know — is that a fact or a guess?"
  • "Whose point of view are we missing — Khrushchev's, a Cuban's?"
  • "What are the advantages and disadvantages of that option?"
  • "How would you know if the solution actually worked — then and now?"
