The Panama Canal, 1903 — a World-Power Question

A Problem-Based Learning unit. Students step into the crisis of 1903, take on a stakeholder's point of view, and work a real, ill-structured question about power, sovereignty, and progress — building from surface to deep to transfer learning. The teacher is a guide, not the answer key.

Driving question: It is 1903. Should the United States build and control a canal across the Isthmus of Panama — and if so, how, and on whose terms?

The three-phase path (do them in order)

Problem-solving is a transfer move — it only works once students have knowledge to reason with. So the problem in Phase 3 is deliberately gated behind Phases 1 and 2.

① Surface

Build the knowledge

Vocabulary, key facts about sea power and the canal race, a jigsaw read, and map work on the isthmus. ~1–2 periods.

 ② Deep

Connect & organize

Benefits–costs concept map, primary sources, competing points of view, and a structured argument. ~1–2 periods.

 ③ Transfer

Solve the problem

Meet the problem, take a stakeholder role, investigate, propose & defend a solution, debrief. ~2–4 periods.
⛔ Gate: don't open the Phase 3 problem until students have finished the surface and deep activities. Meeting the problem too early turns inquiry into guessing.

Big idea & objectives

Big idea: Nations weigh strategic interest, cost, and the rights of others when they decide whether and how to project power — and the choices the United States made in 1903 launched it as a world power while leaving a contested legacy of intervention and sovereignty.

  • Know: why a canal mattered for trade and naval power, how the Panama route was chosen, and what the 1903 secession and treaty actually did.
  • Understand: the same event looks profoundly different from each stakeholder's point of view — expansionist, Colombian, Panamanian, and laborer alike.
  • Do: run the problem-solving and decision-making process end to end — identify the problem, gather information, list and weigh options, choose and defend a solution, and evaluate it (§113.41(c)(31)(B)).

Teacher prep & materials

  • Print the facilitator guide (teacher moves, map of possibilities, role cards, debrief prompts) and the assessment pack (rubric + individual-in-group).
  • Chart paper or a projected space for three shared columns: Hunches · Know · Need-to-Know (the KWHL chart).
  • Vetted sources for investigation (district-approved). Suggested public-access sources are listed in the facilitator guide.
  • No logins, no accounts — every page runs in the browser. Student writing and products live on paper or in tools your district already vets.
⚠️ Honest history: this unit teaches the 1903 secession of Panama from Colombia, the "in perpetuity" Canal Zone concession, and the human cost of construction — including the segregated "gold roll / silver roll" labor system — directly, factually, and respectfully (TEC §28.0022). It also notes the 1977 Torrijos–Carter Treaties that returned the canal to Panama by 1999.
