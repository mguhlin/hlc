Phase 1 — Build the knowledge

Before students can reason about the New Deal problem, they need the raw material: the vocabulary, the facts of the crash and the Depression, and the alphabet agencies. These three activities are fast and front-loaded — the goal is acquisition, not yet analysis.

🎯 By the end of Phase 1 students can use the key vocabulary, explain what caused the Great Depression and how it hurt people, name the major New Deal agencies and the "3 Rs" they served, and read unemployment data across the Hoover and Roosevelt years.
🎟️ Entry ticket — 1-minute warm-up
Vocabulary & feedback · d 0.62§113.41(c)(16)(B), (c)(16)(E)

1 · Word bank & vocabulary sort

Introduce and let students sort the unit vocabulary. Sort twice: first by "words about the crisis" vs. "words about the government's response," then by student-invented categories (feedback on their reasoning).

TermWorking definition
Great Depressionthe severe worldwide economic collapse of the 1930s, the worst in U.S. history
unemploymentthe share of people who want work but cannot find it — about one in four Americans by 1933
relief, recovery, reform — the "3 Rs"the three goals of the New Deal: immediate relief for the suffering, recovery of the economy, and reform so it could not happen again
New DealFranklin Roosevelt's set of programs and agencies to fight the Depression
deficit spendinga government spending more than it collects, borrowing to fund programs
Social Securitythe 1935 program of old-age insurance and aid funded by payroll taxes
laissez-fairethe belief that government should leave the economy largely alone
welfare statea system in which government takes responsibility for citizens' basic economic security
alphabet agenciesthe many New Deal programs known by initials — CCC, WPA, TVA, FDIC, SSA, and more
stakeholderanyone who is affected by a decision or has something at stake in it

Jigsaw method · d 0.92§113.41(c)(16)(C), (c)(28)(A)

2 · Jigsaw reading — From crash to New Deal

Split the class into four expert groups, each studying one topic below, then re-mix into home groups where every topic is represented. Each expert teaches their group. (Jigsaw is one of the highest-leverage surface moves precisely because students must teach.)

🧩 Use the ACE Powered Jigsaw Organizer — experts capture their notes on it before teaching their home group: open the organizer ↗. New to running a jigsaw? See the teacher guides in the facilitator guide.

Sources for each expert group (free, reputable; confirm access through your district — links open in a new tab):

A · The 1929 crash & the causes of the Depression

The stock market crashed in October 1929. Bank failures, overproduction, and shrinking demand turned it into a decade-long depression; by 1933 thousands of banks had closed and a quarter of workers had no job.

📄 Library of Congress · The Great Depression ↗
📄 FDR Presidential Library · Great Depression facts ↗

B · The Dust Bowl & human suffering

Drought and poor farming turned the Southern Plains into the Dust Bowl. Farms failed, families lost everything, and hundreds of thousands migrated west looking for work — the human face of the crisis.

📄 Library of Congress · Voices from the Dust Bowl ↗
📄 Library of Congress · The Great Depression ↗

C · Hoover vs. FDR & the Hundred Days

President Hoover favored limited federal action; voters turned to Franklin Roosevelt in 1932. In his first "Hundred Days" FDR pushed emergency laws through Congress and reassured the nation in radio "fireside chats."

📄 FDR Presidential Library · FDR and the New Deal ↗
📄 FDR Presidential Library · The Fireside Chats ↗

D · The alphabet agencies (CCC, WPA, TVA, FDIC, SSA)

The New Deal built agencies known by their initials: the CCC put young men to work in nature; the WPA built roads and art; the TVA electrified a region; the FDIC insured bank deposits; the SSA created Social Security in 1935.

Check for understanding: each home group writes two sentences answering "Give one cause of the Great Depression, and name one New Deal agency and the 'R' — relief, recovery, or reform — it served."

Direct instruction · d 0.56§113.41(c)(16)(C), (c)(16)(E)

3 · Data work — unemployment and the agencies

Using a simple unemployment table or line graph, students track the jobless rate from the 1929 crash through 1935: roughly 3% in 1929, a peak near 25% in 1933, easing toward the mid-teens by 1935 as New Deal programs took hold. Then students match each major agency — CCC, WPA, TVA, FDIC, SSA — to the "R" it served (relief, recovery, or reform) and to the people it helped.

Quick write: "Unemployment went from ______ in 1929 to about ______ in 1933. One agency that tried to fix this was ______, which served the goal of ______ (relief / recovery / reform) because ______."
