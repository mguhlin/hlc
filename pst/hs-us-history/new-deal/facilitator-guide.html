Facilitator Guide

How to run The New Deal, 1935 as Problem-Based Learning: your role, the pre-planning maps, pacing, role cards, sources, and debrief prompts. The golden rule — guide, don't tell. In PBL the students should feel they, not you, planned the investigation.

Your role by phase

PhaseWhat you doWhat you resist
① SurfaceTeach vocabulary and the facts of the crash, the Depression, and the agencies efficiently; run the jigsaw; check acquisition.Rushing to the problem before facts are secure.
② DeepFacilitate the benefits–costs / 3-Rs map and source routine; model claim + evidence.Giving your own verdict on the New Deal or the sources.
③ TransferRead the problem well; hold the KWHL chart; answer questions with questions; point to sources.Answering the "Need to know" questions yourself.

Running the jigsaw (Phase 1)

The Surface phase uses a four-topic jigsaw (From crash to New Deal) — a high-leverage move (d ≈ 0.92) because every student must teach. The flow: expert groups each study one topic and take notes → students re-mix into home groups with one expert per topic → each expert teaches → an individual check for understanding holds everyone accountable.

Pre-planning · Map of Possibilities

Before teaching, brainstorm every direction the New Deal problem could branch — so you can steer discussion and decide, in advance, which threads are productive and which are too sensitive or off-topic for your class and community.

Center: The New Deal, 1935. Branches: causes of the Depression · relief, recovery & reform · the alphabet agencies · Social Security & its exclusions · deficit spending & the debt · the constitutional fight (Schechter, court-packing) · critics on the right and the left · the lasting welfare state. Mark threads you will encourage and any you will redirect (keep it factual, professional, and age-appropriate — relevant, not inflammatory).

Suggested pacing (5–8 class periods)

  • Period 1: Surface — vocabulary sort + jigsaw.
  • Period 2: Surface data work → Deep benefits–costs / 3-Rs map.
  • Period 3: Deep — primary sources + warm-up argument.
  • Period 4: Transfer — Meet the Problem, roles, Hunches/Know/Need-to-Know.
  • Periods 5–6: Transfer — inquiry & investigation.
  • Periods 7–8: Transfer — build, present, and debrief the 8-part brief.

Facilitation prompts (keep these handy)

  • "What in the record makes you say that?"
  • "Where could we find that out? Which source?"
  • "How do you know — is that a fact or a guess?"
  • "Whose point of view are we missing — an excluded worker's, a justice's?"
  • "What are the advantages and disadvantages of that option?"
  • "How would you know if the solution actually worked — then and now?"

⚠️ Keep sources grade-appropriate and community-appropriate. This unit teaches the New Deal's exclusions, its deficit spending, and the constitutional fight over federal power as history and as a problem-solving process — factual, relevant, and respectful, never inflammatory (TEC §28.0022). Students' outside research should use tools your district already vets.
