The News Delay

Freedom was law long before it reached everyone. Put the steps in order to trace how — and how slowly — the news of freedom traveled to Texas.

I can… explain cause and effect and why news of freedom was delayed.

Look closely

  1. 1863–64Some enslavers move people to Texas, far from Union lines, to keep them enslaved.
  2. Sep 1862President Lincoln issues the preliminary Emancipation Proclamation.
  3. June 1865U.S. Army troops under General Granger reach Galveston and announce freedom.
  4. Apr 1865The Confederacy surrenders; the Civil War ends.
  5. Jan 1863The Proclamation takes effect — but it can be enforced only where the U.S. Army has reached.

Show your thinking

The Emancipation Proclamation took effect in 1863, but Texans were not freed until June 1865. Explain one reason for the delay and name who gained from it.

Teacher notes & alignment

Objective

Students trace cause and effect in the delayed enforcement of emancipation in Texas.

TEKS alignment

Current TEKS: 7.5(C), 8.1(A) — Explain effects of the Civil War and Reconstruction; identify causes and effects of major eras through 1877.

2026 TEKS (fill once crosswalks publish): __________

Answer key

  • Order: preliminary Proclamation (1862) → Proclamation takes effect (1863) → enslavers move people to Texas (1863–64) → Confederate surrender (Apr 1865) → Galveston announcement (June 1865).
  • The Proclamation freed people only where the Army could enforce it; Texas was distant, and some enslavers moved people there to avoid Union control. Enslavers gained free labor during the 2.5-year delay.

Discussion questions

  • Why was a law not enough to make people free in practice?
  • Who had an interest in keeping the news from spreading?
  • How does this delay connect to why Juneteenth is remembered?

Teaching hard history

Teach slavery and emancipation as central to U.S. history, not a side note. Use precise language — enslaved people, enslavers — and keep people, not systems alone, at the center. Center the agency of the freed, and examine the gap between freedom as law and freedom in practice. (Framework: Learning for Justice, Teaching Hard History.)
