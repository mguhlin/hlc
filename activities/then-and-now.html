Invented practice scenario — not a historical record.

Your challenge

  1. Recall · 2 minutes: Say one thing you already know about this kind of evidence.
  2. Model · 4 minutes: Identify a visible detail. Explain how an observation differs from an inference.
  3. Investigate · 6 minutes: Work with a partner on the first two challenges. Point back to the evidence.
  4. Connect · 6 minutes: Each partner explains a different card or part of the display. Teach it to your partner, then connect the details.
  5. Respond · 4 minutes: Make your own claim, evidence, and explanation. Add a limit or a next question.
  6. Revise · 3 minutes: Exchange feedback about one detail. Improve your response and explain what changed.
K–2

Point, draw, or say one idea. Show the detail that helped you.

Grades 3–5

Explain your idea using two details. Use because to connect your evidence.

Grades 6–8

Make a claim, connect two details to it, and identify an unanswered question.

Grades 9–12

Qualify a claim with evidence. Consider another explanation or perspective, and state a limitation.

Your challenge

  1. Put the cards in time order. Which clue tells you what came first?
  2. Compare the first and last cards. Name one change; point to the words or dates supporting it.
  3. Does happening earlier prove an event caused a later one? Explain what extra evidence you would need.
  4. Add a question between two cards. What missing record could help answer it?
  5. Tell the sequence using first, later, and finally. Include a detail and something the cards cannot tell you.
Finish on your own

I think ___. My evidence is ___. It supports my idea because ___.

Write or draw your response here.

Teacher notes & alignment

Look for an accurate detail, a clear connection, and a limit when the task asks for one. Ask students to revise after specific feedback.

Dates establish order; use the wording to distinguish events within the same year. Order alone does not establish a cause. Ask for another record before accepting a causal claim.

1980: The school has one classroom. → 2000: The school has two classrooms. → 2020: A library opens at the school.

Read aloud, enlarge the evidence, rehearse in a home language, and offer pointing or drawing when it preserves the content goal. Assess the selected English language domain separately.
